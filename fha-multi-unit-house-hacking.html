The Play

How House Hacking Actually Works

FHA lets you buy a 2-, 3-, or 4-unit property with 3.5% down as long as you live in one unit for at least a year. The other units' rent offsets your payment - and here's the part that surprises everyone: a portion of the other units' market rent can count as income to help you qualify, so the building helps buy itself. FHA loan limits are also higher for multi-unit properties, which matters at Bay Area prices.

On 3-4 unit properties there's an extra hurdle called the self-sufficiency test - a portion of the building's total market rents has to cover the full payment. It kills a lot of deals in high-price areas, which is why duplexes are often the sweet spot here, and why I run the test before you write an offer, not after.

Questions

Straight Answers

Do rental units really count toward my income?
Yes - on multi-unit owner-occupied purchases, a portion of the other units' documented market rent can be added to your qualifying income, per program rules. It's why buyers qualify for fourplexes they could never afford as single homes.
What is the FHA self-sufficiency test?
On 3-4 unit properties, a percentage of the building's total market rents must cover the entire monthly payment. Duplexes are exempt - one reason they're the East Bay sweet spot. I run this test on any listing before you offer.
How long do I have to live there?
FHA requires owner-occupancy for at least one year in one of the units. After that, keeping it as a pure rental is allowed.
Can I use this with down payment assistance?
Often yes - several assistance programs work on 2-4 unit owner-occupied properties. Stacking DPA with house hacking is an incredibly capital-efficient first purchase - ask me.
