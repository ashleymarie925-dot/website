Solar & Your Mortgage

Leased vs. owned changes everything

Owned panels (cash or paid-off loan) simply add value - no complications. Financed panels often carry a UCC filing that must be addressed in escrow. Leased panels and PPAs must be assumed by the buyer or bought out by the seller - the solar company has its own approval process with its own timeline, and the monthly payment can count in the buyer's debt-to-income ratio. In Brentwood, Antioch and Oakley this comes up on most transactions, and the difference between a smooth close and a blown one is whether the lender starts the solar transfer in week one or discovers it in week four.

Questions

Straight Answers

Do leased solar panels make it harder to buy a house?
Not if handled early. The lease transfer is a process with its own approval - I start it the day you're in contract, and I flag whether the payment counts in your ratios before you offer.
Does a solar lease payment count against my qualification?
Often yes - lease and PPA payments typically count in your debt-to-income ratio, while owned systems don't. This is exactly the kind of detail that changes your real budget.
I'm selling a home with financed panels. What happens?
The panel loan's UCC filing is handled in escrow - usually paid through closing or formally subordinated. Tell your agent to loop me in early and it's painless.
Is there help for adding solar when I buy?
There are financing structures and programs for that - ask me about the current solar program options when we talk.
